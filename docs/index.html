fetch

          
Any field, any source, as of any date. Restatements arrive as separate rows rather than an
          overwrite, so you get both figures and the date each was filed.

        

        

          

            Nine sources behind one call: filings, macro, factors, prices, flow.

          

            as_of is not a filter. It is the index the whole panel is built on.

          

            Every value carries observed_at and known_at, or says it cannot.

          

            No API key, no account, nothing hosted by us.

        

        

          
          

            
            
          

        

        
# What did Apple report for FY2019 — as anyone could see it on New Year's Day 2020?
fetch(entity="AAPL", field="sec:Revenues", fiscal_year=2019, as_of="2020-01-01")

from vintage import Client

v = Client()
row = v.fetch(entity="AAPL", field="sec:Revenues", fiscal_year=2019,
             as_of="2020-01-01")

print(row.value, row.known_at)     # 338500000000 2019-10-31T16:32:00Z
print(row.superseded_by.value)     # 323900000000 — filed later, hidden here

backtest

          
Cross-sectional signal to returns, costs, and an honesty report. Costs are charged on
          turnover always, and no flag turns off the point-in-time index.

        

        

          

            The panel is indexed on known_at. Look-ahead is structurally impossible.

          

            Turnover is always charged. A zero-cost backtest is not a backtest.

          

            A session ledger counts every specification you tried.

          

            The Sharpe is deflated by that count, and cannot be turned off.

        

        

          
          

            
            
          

        

        
# Your 41st idea this session. It remembers the other 40.
backtest(signal="momentum_12_1", universe="dow30", start="2010-01-01")

✓ 3,914 sessions  ·  30 tickers  ·  panel on known_at
  annualized return 14.8%   volatility 17.1%   turnover cost −40 bps/yr
  sharpe            2.14

── honesty report ───────────────────────────────────────────
  specs tried 41   sharpe noise produces 2.29   deflated 0.09
  after 41 trials, a sharpe this high is what noise produces.

        
res = v.backtest(signal="momentum_12_1", universe="dow30",
                  start="2010-01-01", rebalance="monthly")

print(res.sharpe)                  # 2.14
print(res.honesty.trials)          # 41
print(res.honesty.deflated_sharpe) # 0.09
print(res.honesty.verdict)         # "this is what noise produces"

benchmark

          
Your returns against the published factors, from the university that computes them — plus

            331 published predictors, each with its claimed return and t-statistic.

            History runs to July 1926, so the sample is the paper's, not yours.

        

        

          
          

            
            
          

        

        
# Is it you, or is it the factor?
benchmark(returns=my_strategy, against=["ff5", "mom", "openap:Mom12m"])

  alpha (monthly) 0.08%  t = 0.61      mkt-rf beta 0.94

── vs. published claim ──────────────────────────────────────

rep = v.benchmark(returns=my_strategy,
                   against=["ff5", "mom", "openap:Mom12m"])

print(rep.alpha_monthly, rep.alpha_t)   # 0.0008 0.61
print(rep.loadings["mom"])            # 0.71  (t = 6.20)
print(rep.claims["openap:Mom12m"])    # 1.31%/month, t = 3.74

Where the data comes from

    
A century of market history, nine sources, zero API keys.

    
The Fama-French factors start in July 1926 and the SEC filing stream runs to this
    morning — Vintage covers both ends from the same six verbs. Most of these are the primary source,
    not a reseller and not a scraper: the filings come from the regulator that receives them, the macro
    series from the central bank that publishes them, and the factors from the university that computes
    them. Vintage hosts none of it — it connects, normalizes, and preserves vintage.

        Primary · US regulator
        
SEC EDGAR XBRL

        
Every concept every US filer has ever tagged, with the accession number and filing date
        attached to each figure. Restatements arrive as separate rows, not as an overwrite.

      

      

        Primary · US regulator
        
SEC filings stream

        
8-K, 10-K, 10-Q, Form 4 and 13D/G, timestamped to the second EDGAR accepted them. This is
        the clock the rest of the market runs on.

      

      

        Primary · central bank
        
FRED & ALFRED

        
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. ALFRED is the rare archive that keeps first releases,
        so you can ask what CPI looked like that morning rather than after the revisions.

      

      

        Primary · academic
        
Ken French Data Library

        
Dartmouth. FF3, FF5, momentum, daily FF3, and the 49 industry portfolios — the same series
        the papers are written against, from where the authors publish them.

      

      

        Primary · academic
        
Open Source Asset Pricing

        
Chen & Zimmermann's replication of the anomaly zoo: 331 published predictors with the
        return and t-statistic each paper claimed, its sample window, and a definition precise enough
        to implement from. openap:Mom12m returns Jegadeesh-Titman's 1.31%/month, t = 3.74.

      

      

        Primary · US regulator
        
FINRA short sale volume

        
Daily short volume for every consolidated-tape symbol, published after each close and never
        revised. Free, genuinely predictive, and almost nobody glues it. Note it is short volume,
        not short interest — a flow measure, and the response says so.

      

      

        Exchange
        
Coinbase Exchange

        
Crypto OHLCV, no key. A trade print is never restated, so these are more honestly
        point-in-time than equity adjusted closes — but dead tokens are absent entirely, so crypto
        survivorship is worse than equities, not milder.

      

      

        Community
        
ApeWisdom

        
Forum mention ranks across ~15 stock and crypto subreddits. There is no history upstream, so
        every row is stamped with the moment we fetched it. Backtestable history starts the day you
        record it — the only honest version, since anyone selling years of "historical sentiment"
        built it by re-scoring old posts.

      

      

        Third party
        
Yahoo Finance

        
Daily OHLCV and adjusted close, decades deep. Labelled honestly: adjustments are applied
        retroactively, so price history is not fully point-in-time and Vintage says so on every row.

      

    

    
The
    full field-by-field catalogue lists every prefix, dataset and signal with measured coverage
    spans — generated from the registry, so it cannot drift from the code. Counts current as of
    August 2026. Each upstream source keeps its own terms; Vintage redistributes none of it.

  






  

    
Two dates, always

    
Every value carries when it was true and when it was known.

    

      

        
observed_at

        
What period the number describes. Apple's Q4 2019 assets describe September 2019.

      

      

        
known_at

        
When it first became public. You could not have traded on it until the 10-K landed in October.

      

    

    
The backtest panel is indexed on known_at, so any slice of it is
    automatically point-in-time. There is no flag to turn that off. Sources that cannot supply an
    honest known_at are flagged UNKNOWN_VINTAGE rather than given a made-up date.


    

      

        
Six kinds of drift

        
Six ways yesterday's data quietly changed.

        
Each one turns a backtest into a memory of the future. Vintage handles all
        six in the panel index rather than trusting you to remember them.

      

      See the landscape →
    


    

      

        
        
        
        
        
        
      

      

        
Lag

        
The number is true in December. It gets published in February. Anyone who traded on it in
        January was reading a number that did not exist yet.

      

      

        
Restatement

        
The company says "oops, wrong" and changes last year's number. Most feeds keep only the
        correction; Vintage keeps both rows and the date each was filed.

      

      

        
Revision

        
The government keeps fixing old jobs and inflation figures, for years. ALFRED archives the
        first release, so you can ask what the print looked like that morning.

      

      

        
Survivorship

        
Dead companies get deleted. Only the winners are still listed, which makes every historical
        universe quietly flattering. Where Vintage cannot fix it, it says so in the response.

      

      

        
Membership

        
Today's S&P 500 list is not the list from 2005. Backtesting the current index over old
        data buys every stock that was about to be added.

      

      

        
Price adjustment

        
Splits and dividends silently rewrite every price before them, so an adjusted close is not
        what anyone saw. Vintage labels it on every row rather than pretending otherwise.

      

    

    
All six say the same thing: the data you
    have today is not what people saw back then. Point-in-time means showing only what was
    already public that day — and Vintage enforces it in the panel index rather than trusting you to
    remember.
